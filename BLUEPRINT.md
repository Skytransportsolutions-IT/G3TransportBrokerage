# G3 Transport Inc — Website Blueprint

A working first-draft website for **G3 Transport Inc**, a California-based **licensed freight
brokerage** that arranges refrigerated transportation of fresh produce from California to markets
across the United States.

> **Business model matters here.** G3 does not own trucks or employ drivers &mdash; it books freight
> with vetted carriers. Every page is written from that position: capacity instead of fleet, carrier
> vetting instead of driver hiring, broker authority and a BMC-84 surety bond instead of cargo
> insurance on owned equipment. If G3 also holds carrier authority and runs its own trucks, say so
> and an "our fleet" section can go back in.

Static HTML/CSS/JS. No build step, no dependencies. Open `index.html` in a browser to view it.

## Brand

Pulled directly from the supplied logos.

| Token | Value | Use |
|---|---|---|
| Green | `#0E6B3A` | Primary brand, links, icons |
| Deep green | `#08381F` | Hero, dark sections, footer |
| Red | `#CE1F2A` | Calls to action, accents |
| Cream | `#F6F4EF` | Page background |
| Ink | `#16181A` | Body text |

Type: **Barlow Condensed** (headings, echoes the logo lettering) + **Inter** (body). Both free via Google Fonts.

Logo usage as built — change on request:
- `logo-primary.jpg` (horizontal truck mark) → header + footer
- `logo-badge.jpg` (circular badge) → Coverage page; also the source for the favicon
- `logo-seal.jpg` (produce division seal) → Home "why us"
- `logo-stacked.jpg` → About page

The four supplied files are fully opaque (no transparency) and sit on a near-white paper ground,
so the web copies were resized and converted to JPEG — 7 MB of artwork down to under 800 KB — and
are displayed with `mix-blend-mode: multiply` so the ground disappears against the cream page.
The favicon set (`favicon.ico`, `assets/img/favicon-512.png`, `assets/img/apple-touch-icon.png`)
was generated by square-cropping the circular badge.

> **Worth requesting from whoever made the logos: transparent PNG or SVG masters.** Vector art
> would render sharper at every size, work on any background without the blend-mode trick, and
> allow a cleanly redrawn favicon instead of a scaled-down badge whose text is unreadable at 32px.

## Everything highlighted in yellow is a placeholder

Placeholder values are wrapped in `<span class="tbd">` and render with a yellow highlight, so they
are impossible to miss during review. Nothing invented has been presented as fact — no phone
number, address, DOT/MC number, insurance limit, fleet size, transit time, pay rate or testimonial
is real. `blueprint.html` lists them all in one place.

The short version of what the client needs to supply:

**Contact & legal** — phone numbers, emails, street address, office vs after-hours coverage, **broker MC #**, USDOT #, domain name.

**Operations** — carrier network size, temperature range and payload you source, real transit times per region, which growing regions are actually covered, Yuma/border coverage, whether to promote dry backhauls.

**Commercial** — accessorial charges (or drop that table), shipper payment terms, **BMC-84 surety bond amount and provider**, contingent cargo and general liability limits, carrier vetting tools (RMIS / Highway / Carrier411), year founded.

**Carrier terms** — quick pay percentage and timing, standard net terms, carrier insurance minimums (auto liability and cargo), whether reefer breakdown cover is required, detention and layover policy.

**Content** — founding story, real testimonials with permission, photography, team bios (or drop the section).

## Photography and video

The site carries 23 photographs and two background video clips so it can be judged as a finished
piece rather than a wireframe. **All of it is licensed stock (Pexels License: free for commercial
use, no attribution required) and none of it shows G3 equipment.** Full inventory, source IDs and
licensing notes are in `CREDITS.md`.

How it is built:

- **Hero video** — 12s highway clip, 1440×810, muted, looping, 1.8 MB.
- **Field video** — 9s leafy-greens clip behind the "from the field to the dock" band, 0.7 MB.
- Both sit over poster frames extracted from the clips themselves, so the section looks correct
  before (or instead of) playback.
- Videos **pause when scrolled off screen** and are **disabled entirely** under
  `prefers-reduced-motion`. Autoplay failure degrades to the poster image.
- Photos are cropped per slot and sized to roughly 2x their display width (700-1600px), lazy-loaded
  below the fold, with explicit `width`/`height` so nothing shifts as the page loads.
- Whole site is 6.9 MB on disk including all media; a first visit to the home page pulls about 2 MB,
  most of it the hero clip.

> **Replace the stock with real photography before launch.** For a brokerage the honest shot list is
> different from a carrier's: your office and freight desk, headshots of the reps a shipper will
> actually speak to, and — with written permission — carrier equipment on your loads. Never caption a
> stock truck as "our truck". Three cautions: the driver portrait shows an identifiable person and
> should not stay on a live commercial site, no replacement photo should contain another company's
> branding, and a brokerage showing trucks it does not own is a claim worth being careful about.

## Not built yet

- **Forms are front-end only.** They validate and show a confirmation, but send nothing. Wire to a
  mail endpoint (Formspree, Netlify Forms, or a small server handler) before launch, ideally with an
  auto-reply to the sender.
- **Route map graphic** on Coverage is a placeholder.
- **Google Map embed** on Contact needs the confirmed address.
- **Privacy policy and terms** — footer links go nowhere.
- **Analytics, Google Business Profile, sitemap.xml, robots.txt** — not set up. Meta titles and
  descriptions are drafted per page.
- **Spanish version** — not built. Worth deciding on early, since it affects structure.
